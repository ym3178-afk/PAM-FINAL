11

Rhetorical argument

The route is not found.
It is produced.

Every route is the outcome of decisions about what counts as time, cost, safety, accessibility, and efficiency.

Platform presents ONE ROUTE ONE ETA

A clean interface compresses waiting, risk, slope, access restrictions, and rider judgment into a single line.

DESIGN
AS ARGUMENT
Project reveals MULTIPLE ROUTES MULTIPLE COSTS

Different definitions of efficiency produce different spatial realities, while incomplete evidence remains visible.

Time

Platform ETA often foregrounds moving time while restaurant waiting and campus handoff delay remain less visible.

“Change the definition of cost, and the ‘optimal’ route changes.”
Design position

Route visualization should disclose the assumptions and exclusions behind its calculation instead of presenting one result as inevitable.

The challenge

The difficult part is not generating more routes.
It is producing evidence strong enough to explain what they mean.

The next phase must deepen validation, relationships, technical clarity, and visual communication without allowing the project to expand beyond one focused delivery case.

Challenge 01Data and access
Current gap

Platform dispatch logic, historical rider traces, pricing rules, and performance data are not publicly available.
